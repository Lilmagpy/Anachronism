# The Great Khan, 1206 — sources of the scenario and what is uncertain

The scenario (`src/anachronism/content/packs/east_asia/`, files ending `_1206`, and
`scenarios/great_khan.yaml`) was written by Claude from general historical knowledge
(standard surveys of the Mongol empire, the Jin, Xia and Southern Song, Goryeo and Kamakura
Japan). It aims to be plausible and recognisable, not a scholarly reconstruction. Please
correct anything wrong.

## Fairly solid
- The moment: the kurultai of 1206 by the Onon river, where Temujin was proclaimed
  Genghis Khan after uniting the Mongol, Kerait, Naiman, Merkit and Tatar tribes.
- The powers: the Tangut Western Xia (capital Xingqing), the Jurchen Jin (capital Zhongdu,
  today's Beijing), the Southern Song (capital Lin'an, today's Hangzhou), Goryeo (capital
  Kaegyong, with the Choe military family ruling behind the king), the Kamakura shogunate,
  the Qara Khitai (Western Liao) in Central Asia, the Uyghurs of Qocho and the kingdom of
  Dali in Yunnan.
- The Ongut sided with Genghis Khan (1204); the Uyghur idiqut submitted in 1209.
- The Kaixi war: the Song attacked the Jin in 1206 and failed.
- The Mongol campaigns: Xia (1205-1209, conquered 1227), the Jin (from 1211; Zhongdu fell
  in 1215; the Jin ended in 1234), the Qara Khitai (1218), Goryeo (1231-1259), Dali (1253)
  and the Song (fell in 1279).
- Successors: Ogedei, Guyuk, Mongke and Kublai; the Jin emperors Zhangzong, Wanyan
  Yongji, Xuanzong and Aizong; the Xia emperors Huanzong, Xiangzong (from 1206),
  Shenzong and Xianzong; the Song emperors Ningzong, Lizong and Duzong.

## Estimates and simplifications
- **Populations** are rough: the Mongol tribes about 1 million; Jin about 38 million (its
  1207 census counted over 50 million); the Song about 55 million; Xia about 3 million.
- **Mobilisation** (`martial_bp`): steppe peoples put nearly every adult man on a horse,
  so the Mongols count fifteen times the usual share of their people under arms; the Jin
  (half) and Song (two fifths) count less, for their famously weakened armies. These are
  game numbers, tuned so that a determined Mongol player can do what history did - slowly.
- **Inner Asia** is divided into large, thinly peopled provinces; a few links across the
  steppe, the Gobi and the Tibetan plateau are added by hand where the map leaves gaps.
- **Rulers**: Wanyan Yongji's and the Gurkhan Zhilugu's ages are guesses; Dali's king in
  1206 is left unnamed.
- **Faiths**: "Tengri and the spirits" for the Mongols; the Church of the East (Nestorian
  Christianity) for the Ongut; Buddhism across Xia, Jin, Goryeo, Japan, the Qara Khitai,
  the Uyghurs and Dali; Neo-Confucianism for the Song court.
- **Technology**: the Song and Jin have gunpowder weapons, the compass and paper money;
  the Mongols write with the Uyghur script (adopted 1204) and have little else beyond the
  horse and the bow. The Mongol postal relay (the yam, 1220s) is left for the player.
