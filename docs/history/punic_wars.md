# Rome and Carthage, 264 BC — sources of the scenario and what is uncertain

Written by Claude from general historical knowledge (standard modern surveys of the
Hellenistic world and the Roman Republic, following Polybius and Livy as usually told). It aims
to be plausible and recognisable, not a scholarly reconstruction. Please correct anything wrong.

## Fairly solid
- 264 BC: the Mamertines of Messana appeal to both Rome and Carthage; the First Punic War
  begins. Rome's consul that year is Appius Claudius Caudex.
- Hiero II rules Syracuse; Antigonus II Gonatas rules Macedon; Ptolemy II Philadelphus rules
  Egypt from Alexandria; Antiochus I Soter rules the Seleucid empire; Alexander II rules
  Epirus after Pyrrhus's death (272 BC); Philetaerus holds Pergamon; Nicomedes I rules
  Bithynia.
- The Chremonidean War (about 267–261 BC): Athens and Sparta, backed by Ptolemy II, fight
  Macedon, which holds Corinth.
- Rome has the Via Appia and the Aqua Appia (312 BC); Alexandria has the Museum and Library.

## Estimates and simplifications
- **Populations** are rough, scaled from common modern estimates (Roman Italy about 4 million
  with allies, Egypt 4 million, the Seleucid lands in the map about 7 million).
- **Rulers left blank** where the sources or my knowledge are unclear: Carthage (ruled by
  magistrates and a council), Numidia, Kush, the Greek leagues and republics.
- **Sparta**: Areus I died in 265 BC; his son Acrotatus is shown as king.
- **Pontus**: Ariobarzanes's dates (about 266–250 BC) are uncertain.
- **Cisalpine Gauls** (Insubres and Boii) are shown as one civilisation; many Celtic,
  Iberian, Germanic, Thracian and Scythian peoples are shown as unclaimed lands.
- **Borders** are grown by the game over the real terrain (D-056), about 20 km accurate.
- The Caspian Sea has no shore provinces in this scenario.
