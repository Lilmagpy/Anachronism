# The Three Kingdoms of Korea, AD 400 — sources of the scenario and what is uncertain

The scenario (`src/anachronism/content/packs/east_asia/`, files ending `_400`, and
`scenarios/three_kingdoms.yaml`) was written by Claude from general historical knowledge
(modern surveys of early Korean, Japanese and Six Dynasties Chinese history, and the
Gwanggaeto Stele as usually read). It aims to be plausible and recognisable, not a scholarly
reconstruction. Please correct anything wrong.

## Fairly solid
- Goguryeo under Gwanggaeto the Great (r. 391–413), capital at Gungnae on the Yalu (the
  move to Pyongyang came in 427); the Taehak academy founded in 372.
- Baekje (capital Hanseong/Wirye on the Han River), humbled by Goguryeo in 396; allied with
  the Wa and in contact with the Eastern Jin court.
- Silla (Seorabeol/Gyeongju) under King Naemul, attacked by the Wa in 399–400 and rescued by
  a Goguryeo army in 400, after which it was Goguryeo's client.
- The Gaya league on the Nakdong, famous for iron; Geumgwan Gaya at Gimhae.
- In China: Northern Wei (Pingcheng) had just taken the Hebei plain from Later Yan (397–398);
  Later Yan held out in Liaoxi; Southern Yan held Shandong; Later Qin held Chang'an and
  Luoyang (Kumarajiva arrived in 401); Eastern Jin ruled the south from Jiankang; the Rouran
  dominated the Mongolian steppe.

## Estimates and simplifications
- **Populations** are rough guesses; the sources give almost nothing reliable for Korea and
  Japan at this date.
- **Wa (Yamato)**: the extent of Yamato authority around 400 is debated; the scenario gives it
  a loose hegemony over western Japan and the Kanto. Nintoku's reign dates are legendary.
- **Liaodong** is given to Later Yan (it changed hands around 400–405).
- **Leaders**: Baekje's Asin, Silla's Naemul, Later Yan's Murong Sheng (d. 401), Northern
  Wei's Daowu, Later Qin's Yao Xing, Southern Yan's Murong De, Eastern Jin's Emperor An (a
  figurehead under the generals), the Rouran's Shelun.
- **Technology baselines** are broad-brush (for instance, stirrups in Goguryeo tomb murals;
  paper and schools spreading from China).

## Scripts (what the rivals intend)
Wa's attack on Silla, Later Yan's strike at Goguryeo, Baekje and Wa's northern attack (404),
the Silla–Baekje alliance (433), Silla's absorption of Gaya (532/562), Northern Wei's wars
with Later Qin (402), the Rouran (429) and the last Yan state (436), and Liu Yu's conquests
of Southern Yan (410) and Later Qin (417). Each fires only if its conditions hold.
