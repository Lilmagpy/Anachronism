# Japan's Warring States, 1560 — sources of the scenario and what is uncertain

The scenario (`src/anachronism/content/packs/east_asia/`, files ending `_1560`, and
`scenarios/sengoku.yaml`) was written by Claude from general historical knowledge (standard
surveys of the Sengoku period, the Imjin War and the late Ming). It aims to be plausible and
recognisable, not a scholarly reconstruction. Please correct anything wrong.

## Fairly solid
- The moment: May 1560, just before Okehazama, where Oda Nobunaga's small force killed
  Imagawa Yoshimoto, who was marching on Kyoto.
- The great clans and their heartlands: Oda (Owari), Imagawa (Suruga, Totomi, Mikawa),
  Takeda (Kai, Shinano), Uesugi (Echigo), Hojo (Sagami, Musashi), Date (Mutsu), Asakura
  (Echizen), Saito (Mino), Miyoshi (the capital region), the Ashikaga shogun in Kyoto,
  Mori and Amago in the west, Otomo and Shimazu in Kyushu, Chosokabe in Tosa.
- The Kai–Sagami–Suruga pact (1554) binding Takeda, Hojo and Imagawa; the Takeda–Uesugi
  wars at Kawanakajima; the Mori–Amago war; the Miyoshi and the shogunate.
- Muskets arrived with the Portuguese at Tanegashima in 1543; Jesuits from 1549.
- Joseon Korea (metal movable type, Hangul) and the Ming (the Jiajing emperor, wokou
  pirates); Ryukyu as a Ming tributary trading kingdom.

## Estimates and simplifications
- **Provinces** are grouped (a Japanese "province" here is often two or three real ones);
  many small lords are left as unowned land (Omi, Ise, Kaga's Ikko league, Harima...).
- **Populations** are rough: Japan about 12 million; Korea about 9 million.
- **The Ming** appear only as their northern and coastal provinces within reach, with far
  fewer people than the real empire (about 150 million), so that Japan and Korea can be
  won; this is a deliberate game simplification.
- **Otomo Sorin** is treated as Christian from the start (he protected the Jesuits from
  1551 and was baptised in 1578).
- **Technology**: the game's library has no separate "musket"; gunpowder stands for it.

## Scripts (what the rivals intend)
Imagawa's march on Kyoto, Kawanakajima, the Takeda breaking the pact (1568), Kenshin's siege
of Odawara, the Mori taking Izumo (1566), the Eiroku incident (1565), Saito against Oda,
Shimazu against Otomo (1578), and Chosokabe against the Mori. Each fires only if its
conditions hold.
