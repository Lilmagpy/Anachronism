# The Mauryan dawn, 321 BC — sources of the scenario and what is uncertain

The scenario (`src/anachronism/content/packs/east_asia/`, files ending `_321`, and
`scenarios/maurya.yaml`) was written by Claude from general historical knowledge (standard
surveys of the Nanda and Maurya empires, Alexander's Indian campaign and early Tamil
history). It aims to be plausible and recognisable, not a scholarly reconstruction. Please
correct anything wrong.

## Fairly solid
- Alexander turned back at the Beas (326 BC) and died in Babylon (323 BC), leaving
  garrisons and satraps on the Indus (Eudemus, Peithon), who had withdrawn by about 317 BC.
- The Nanda dynasty ruled the Ganges plain from Pataliputra, famous for its wealth and
  army and unpopular for its taxes; Dhana Nanda was the last king.
- Chandragupta Maurya, guided by the brahmin Chanakya (Kautilya), overthrew the Nandas
  around 321 BC; he fought Seleucus I (about 305 BC), who ceded the lands west of the
  Indus for 500 elephants; his son Bindusara and grandson Ashoka followed.
- Kalinga stayed independent until Ashoka's war (about 261 BC); the Chola, Pandya and
  Chera kingdoms and Lanka (Anuradhapura) traded and fought in the far south.

## Estimates and simplifications
- **Dates are disputed**: Chandragupta's rise is put anywhere from 324 to 321 BC; his age
  (19 here) and Dhana Nanda's are guesses.
- **Populations** are rough: the Nanda realm about 11 million; all of India perhaps 20-30
  million.
- **Mobilisation** (`martial_bp`): Chandragupta's army four times the usual share of his
  people (a war band built for conquest); the Nandas under the usual share, for a vast but
  disaffected army. Game numbers, tuned so a bold Mauryan player can win - not easily.
- **The Tamil, Kalingan and Sinhalese rulers** of 321 BC are mostly unknown and left
  unnamed (Lanka's Mutasiva is from the chronicles, and his date is uncertain).
- **Faiths**: Vedic religion for the Mauryas (Chanakya was a brahmin); Jainism for the
  Nandas and Kalinga (both are linked with Jain tradition); Buddhism is left out at the
  start, since its great spread came with Ashoka.
- **Map**: India is on the East Asia map; the Indian seas (Arabian Sea, Laccadive Sea, Bay
  of Bengal) were added for it. Arachosia and Gedrosia lie off the map's western edge.
