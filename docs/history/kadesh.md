# Egypt and the Hittites, 1275 BC — sources of the scenario and what is uncertain

Written by Claude from general historical knowledge of the Late Bronze Age (the Amarna and
Ramesside periods as told in standard modern surveys). Plausible and recognisable, not a
scholarly reconstruction; dates of this age are uncertain by a decade or more.

## Fairly solid
- Ramesses II (reigned from about 1279 BC) fights the Hittite king Muwatalli II at Kadesh
  in about 1274 BC; Kadesh and Amurru are contested between them.
- Adad-nirari I rules Assyria and has defeated Hanigalbat (the remnant of Mitanni), whose
  king Wasashatta rebels; Kadashman-Turgu rules Kassite Babylon.
- Alaksandu of Wilusa (Troy) made a treaty with Muwatalli II; the Mycenaean Greeks
  (Ahhiyawa) hold Millawanda (Miletus).
- Ugarit's scribes use a 30-sign cuneiform alphabet.

## Estimates and simplifications
- **Populations** are rough (Egypt about 3–4 million).
- **Rulers left blank**: Mycenae (no king's name is known), Alashiya, Ugarit.
- **Elam**: Untash-Napirisha's reign is usually dated from about 1275 BC.
- **Benteshina of Amurru** changed sides around the time of Kadesh.
- Egypt's Nubian (Wawat and Kush) and Canaanite provinces are shown as Egyptian; the many
  vassal city-states of Syria and Canaan are merged into their overlord's provinces.
- Europe's Bronze Age peoples are shown as unclaimed lands.
